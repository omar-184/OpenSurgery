# OpenSurgery

A general surgery reference built entirely from textbooks. Every clinical statement cites the book and chapter it came from, and every question in the question bank was taken word for word from those books.

## How a page is put together

Disease topics all use the same eleven sections in the same order: Summary, Definition, Pathophysiology, Clinical features, Aetiology, Diagnosis, Scoring and Severity, Treatment and Management, Surgeries, Complications, Prognosis. Topics about a procedure or about perioperative care swap in headings that fit what the books describe. Where the sources say nothing under a heading, the heading is dropped rather than padded out.

Nothing is written from memory. Each fact ends with a numbered citation to the book and chapter it came from, and every page closes with its reference list in IEEE style.

## UK guidance

Some pages carry notes from NICE, the Royal College of Surgeons, specialty associations, national screening programmes and national audits. They sit apart from the textbook material, and each one links to the document it came from. The Consultant view shows them woven into the article; the Resident / trainee view hides them and leaves a textbook-only article.

## Question bank

Every specialty has two modes:

- **Practice** has no clock. Answer a question, press *Check answer*, and the correct option turns green, a wrong choice turns red, and the book's explanation appears with its source.
- **Exam** runs one clock for the whole paper, a minute per question, counted down continuously. Choose 10, 20, 40 or all of a specialty's questions, move freely between them, and flag any to come back to. The paper submits itself when the clock reaches zero.

Both show progress and mark the correct choice on the results page. Questions the books give no answer key for are shown as self-check and left out of the score.

## Reading tools

- **Search** every topic from the home page or the toolbar, with results as you type.
- **Marks** pick out the key figures on a page; switch them off for plain text.
- **Annotate** to highlight in four colours, underline, strike through or add notes. Annotations are saved to your profile.
- **Progress** shows the topics you have read, the quizzes you have attempted and your average score.
- **Light or dark** theme, remembered between visits.

## Signing in

The home page and About page are open to everyone. Reading the library and the question bank needs an account, made with Google or an email address. An account stores your email, display name, academic level, country, annotations, and which topics and quizzes you have opened. No clinical or health data about you is collected, and nothing is shared with third parties.

## What it does not claim

- **Not clinical advice.** It is built for revision and reference. What you do belongs to you, your seniors and your local protocols.
- **Not guaranteed current.** Guidelines get revised. A page reflects its sources as they read when it was written. Check the live guideline.
- **Not peer reviewed.** The text has been checked against the sources it cites. No specialist panel has read it.
- **Not complete.** Several general surgery topics are still missing.

## Found a mistake?

Wrong matters more than missing. If something does not match the source it cites, or a figure or threshold looks off, [open an issue](https://github.com/omar-184/OpenSurgery/issues) and say which page and which sentence. Fixes get checked back against the source text.

## About this repository

This repository holds the generated site, published with GitHub Pages. Every file in it is rebuilt from markdown sources on each release, so a change made to the HTML here would be overwritten. Report corrections as issues instead.
