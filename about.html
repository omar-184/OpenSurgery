About OpenSurgery

A general surgery reference built entirely from seven textbooks. Every clinical statement carries a citation to the book and chapter it came from, and every question in the question bank was taken from those books rather than written here.

How a page is put together

Disease topics follow the same eleven sections in the same order — Summary, Definition, Pathophysiology, Clinical features, Etiology, Diagnosis, Scoring and Severity, Treatment and Management, Surgeries, Complications, Prognosis. Procedure and supportive-care topics substitute headings that match what the sources actually describe. Where the books say nothing about a section, the section is left out rather than filled in.

Facts are not paraphrased from memory. Each one is traced to text found in one of the seven books below, and the inline citation names the chapter it came from.

The seven source textbooks

  • Bailey & Love 28e — Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery, 28th ed.
  • Sabiston 22e — Sabiston Textbook of Surgery, 22nd ed.
  • Maingot's 13e — Maingot's Abdominal Operations, 13th ed.
  • Browse's 6e — Browse's Introduction to the Symptoms and Signs of Surgical Disease, 6th ed.
  • Oxford Handbook 5e — Oxford Handbook of Clinical Surgery, 5th ed.
  • Schwartz's ABSITE — Schwartz's Principles of Surgery: ABSITE and Board Review
  • ABSITE Review 2022 — The ABSITE Review, 2022

UK guidance

Some pages carry additional notes drawn from NICE, the Royal College of Surgeons, specialty associations, national screening programmes and national audits. These are kept separate from the textbook material and can be switched off with the UK guidelines toggle beside the page title; with the toggle off, the page reads as a textbook-only article. Each note links out to the source document.

What this site does not claim

  • It is not clinical advice. It is a study and revision reference. Clinical decisions belong to you, your seniors and your local protocols.
  • It is not necessarily current. Guidelines are revised continually. A page reflects the sources as they read when it was written, and the footer date says when this copy was last built — not that every guideline was re-checked that day. Always confirm against the live guideline before acting.
  • It is not peer reviewed. The content has been checked for agreement with its cited sources, but it has not been reviewed by a specialist panel.
  • It is not exhaustive. Several general-surgical topics are not covered yet, and the question bank draws on one of the seven books.

Found a mistake?

Errors matter more here than gaps do. If a statement does not match the source it cites, or a figure or threshold looks wrong, open an issue and name the page and the sentence. Corrections are made against the source text, not from memory.
